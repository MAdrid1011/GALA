# NCU launch-signature plan

`python -m gala_sim.tools.gpu_ncu_plan --config <yaml> --nsys-profile <json> ... --stability-nsys-profile <json> ... --output <json>` validates two complete sets of frozen representative NSYS inventories and creates a measured-counter plan. Non-range selection order must match exactly by iteration, stage, call, kernel ordinal, name, and block. Grid dimensions remain measured workload state because the Gaussian population can diverge across valid GPU training runs. Configured range stages require stable range call identities but may change launch multiplicity and exact signatures. The formal runner rejects a plan without passed repeat stability. Invocation groups are bounded by the configured job count; the Chest campaign uses enough groups to isolate costly kernel-name/ordinal cross products. After NCU runs, `python -m gala_sim.tools.gpu_ncu_plan --plan <plan.json> --ncu-profile <json> ... --output <evidence.json>` validates measured CSV-derived profiles against every required launch and signature. Use `gpu_ncu_runner preflight` for the short, first-window NCU overhead/utilization gate; it deliberately uses common metrics rather than full-run invocation ordinals.

The signature retains stage, demangled kernel name, grid, and block. Multiplicity remains separate for every representative iteration. First, middle, and last observed occurrences are selected to test counter reuse; a signature may be multiplied by its exact NSYS multiplicity only when not every occurrence is measured and the sampled counters agree. When every occurrence is measured, differing counters are retained and summed per launch instead of being reused.

NCU runs with `--profile-from-start off` while `stage_runner` opens CUDA Profiler API windows at all frozen representative iterations. Stable stages use invocation jobs. Stages listed in `range_capture_stages` are excluded before invocation numbering and are captured in a separate job through exact process-wide `gala_ncu_stage` start/end range expressions. The range job profiles and retains every actual launch, derives exact stage/name/grid/block signatures from that NCU run, and performs no multiplicity expansion. New or missing signatures are valid dynamic workload outcomes inside the frozen range calls; launches outside those exact calls remain a failure. This split is backed by a same-process NCU probe showing that NVTX filtering precedes invocation numbering.

The plan groups the remaining kernel names and invocation ordinals into the configured maximum number of complete-training jobs. A single-kernel group whose selected launch count exceeds `maximum_single_kernel_group_launch_count` is partitioned into contiguous actual invocation-ordinal sets before any optional merging; protected partitions are never merged back together. Group merging may measure additional deterministic launches. Invocation jobs bind each launch to its stable semantic position and block shape, retain both the planned NSYS grid and measured NCU grid, and reject name, order, block, or multiplicity drift. A range job binds every observed launch to the exact configured stage call and retains it for counters and SASS classification. `gpu_profile_artifacts ncu-job` also binds the NCU process ID, stage-profile process ID, campaign hash, plan hash, and job index. The generated arguments do not use `--launch-count` or `--kill`, and every official process must naturally execute the complete training path.

The plan is not performance evidence. It remains ineligible until counters, dynamic SASS semantics, local calibration, and AGX Orin calibration pass their gates.
